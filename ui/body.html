Guides for the dashboard, one topic per page or feature. Pick a card below or use the list on the left — more topics appear here as the rest of the UI gets its guides written.

Economic Advisor · Help

The coloured bar on the left of each card is the priority rail. Its fill height is that finding's priority score relative to the single highest-scoring finding in the current scan — so a full rail is the most urgent thing found, and a short rail is comparatively minor. The rail stays fixed to the full findings list even when you filter by type, so a card's height never shifts just because you switched tabs. Sort order and rail height always agree: read top-to-bottom for rank, or glance at the rails for how big the gap is between one finding and the next.

Overflow Risk

Flags a station whose production surplus will fill its cargo bay within 24 in-game hours. If a station makes more of something than it consumes or ships out, that surplus piles up in storage — once it caps, the excess production is simply wasted (nothing more can be stored). Priority is weighted by both the value being produced and how soon it caps, so an urgent, high-value overflow ranks above a slow, low-value one.

Market Opportunity

Matches a surplus ware at one of your stations against a nearby NPC station that genuinely wants more of it (its buy offer's desired stock target exceeds what it currently has — not just "willing to buy," but "actively short"). Ranked by estimated credits/hour, discounted by how many jumps away the buyer is — a big opportunity three jumps out scores lower than a smaller one next door.

Pricing Gap

Compares what you're currently charging on your own posted sell offers against what a reachable NPC station is paying for the same ware. If someone nearby pays more than your listed price, this flags the gap and estimates the one-time gain if you rerouted your current stock there instead.

Idle Hauler

Catches a Freighter/Transport sitting nearly empty (under 15% cargo fill) while its home station is actively overproducing something. The ship has capacity, the station has product — the mismatch is the finding. Priority scales with how much capacity is sitting unused.

Military Advisor · Help

The coloured bar on the left of each card is the priority rail, and it works exactly as on the Economic tab — fill height is the finding's score relative to the single highest-scoring military finding this scan. Military scores are threat points (ship counts weighted by combat role, verdict and distance), a different unit from the economy's credits/hour — which is why each tab is ranked separately and rail heights are never comparable across the two.

A card showing a swords icon carries counter advice: hover it for weapon and refit suggestions matched to that particular force's make-up — strike-craft swarms, capital hulls, and shield-heavy versus hull-heavy fleets each get their own counsel.

Hostile Presence verdicts
Undefended

You have nothing armed in the sector — anything you own there is exposed.

Outmatched

Simulating the fight both ways, they break your defence at least twice as fast as you could break theirs (or your ships can't hurt them at all).

Contested

The engagement could go either way. Fleets the scan couldn't assess also land here — missing data is never scored as harmless.

Covered

Your present force should hold without reinforcement.

Hostile Presence

Flags hostile-faction ships operating in a sector where you own a station, within reach of your position. Hostility is decided by reputation (−25 or below), so a faction you've personally angered counts just as much as the Xenon. Both sides' fitted loadouts are compared — sustained damage output against hull and shield pools — to produce the verdict above. Priority scales with ship count (a combat hull counts four times a freighter), worsens with the verdict, and falls off with distance.

Tracking Mismatch

Their force is mostly strike craft (S/M hulls), while most of your defenders' firepower physically can't track targets that small — plasma turrets never connect with fighters; flak, pulse and guided missiles do. Fires only when the mismatch is one-sided: at least 60% of their ships are small against less than 25% of your damage able to hit them, and fewer than 4 craft never counts as a swarm.

Outranged

Hostile capital ships (L/XL) out-reach every defender you have in the sector by 25% or more — the standoff-bombardment setup, where your ships take fire for the whole approach before they can reply. Only capital hulls count on their side: a fighter with a long-range gun still has to close, but a destroyer parks at maximum range and pounds. Priority is the kilometres of standoff times the platforms that can exploit it.

Force Build-Up

Hostile hardware value in a sector has risen every scan for at least three scans and at least doubled overall. A raid arrives, fights and leaves — only staging climbs steadily, so this is the early warning for an invasion. It's also the one military finding not gated by distance: the threat is to the station in that sector, which doesn't get less threatened because you flew away. Priority scales with fleet size and how fast it's growing.

Damaged Ship

A combat ship flying below 75% hull without being docked anywhere. Shields regenerate on their own; hull damage persists between fights, so it's the signal that a repair stop is overdue. Priority is the missing hull points in absolute terms — a battered destroyer outranks a battered fighter because it costs more to lose and takes longer to replace. Docked damaged ships are assumed to be already handled.

Trader Advisor · Help

The priority rail works the same as the Economic tab — fill height is a finding's score relative to the single highest-scoring trader finding this scan. Unlike Economic (which reacts to one station's current surplus or cargo state), these five rules reason at the galaxy or fleet level: where to expand, where the biggest cross-sector spread is, and whether the fleet and bank balance are actually being put to work.

Station Siting

Flags a discovered sector with a mineable resource deposit and no player station anywhere in it yet. Fires regardless of demand — low demand for the resource is not a reason to skip the suggestion — but calls out reachable unmet NPC demand explicitly when it's strong, so you know when a build is backed by a ready market versus a bet on future use.

Galaxy Arbitrage

Finds the largest reachable buy/sell spread for a ware across NPC stations galaxy-wide — independent of anything you currently own or stock. Both the cheap seller and the paying buyer must be within reach and on speaking terms with you; priority discounts for the total round-trip distance.

Stranded Delivery

A courier has been holding cargo it picked up with no resolved delivery destination for an unusually long time. Priority weighs both how long it's been stranded and the value of the cargo it's sitting on.

Idle Trade Capital

Your banked credits are high while only a small share of your fleet is doing any trading. One finding per scan, scaled by both how much is banked and how low the trading share is.

Reputation Locked

A faction's reachable territory holds a worthwhile ware trade, but your reputation with them is too low to trade at all. Highlights the single best-value ware per blocked faction so improving relations has a concrete payoff attached.
