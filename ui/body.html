Help

Guides for the dashboard, one topic per page or feature. Pick a card below or use the list on the left — more topics appear here as the rest of the UI gets its guides written.

Overview · Help

Everything on this tab is a snapshot of the currently loaded scan — switching scans in the top-right picker redraws all of it, nothing here is historical (for that, see the Trends topic).

Empire Snapshot

Ten cards: Credits (your current bank balance), Total Ships, Stations owned, Hostiles Present (enemy NPC ships currently sitting in a sector where you own a station — turns red once any exist), Waiting (ships currently sitting on the "Waiting" order — turns amber once any exist), and your career stats from the Captains Log — Trade Rank, Trade Score, Fight Rank, Fight Score, and Ships Destroyed. Hostile Hulls (captured ships with a hostile-faction origin) and Waiting also drive the number on the topbar's Alerts badge: one point for each that's non-zero, so a "2" there means both conditions are active — see the Alerts tab for the Hostile Hulls detail, since it's no longer one of these cards.

Fleet by Role

Every player ship bucketed by its assigned role (Trader, Miner, Fighter, and so on) and counted, sorted highest count first. A quick read on what your fleet is actually built to do, independent of hull size.

Fleet by Order

Every player ship bucketed by its current behaviour order (Trade Auto, Patrol, Waiting, Mine, and so on), also sorted by count. Each order gets a fixed icon and colour so the same order reads the same way here as it does on the Fleet tab's Order column — a fleet with a large "Waiting" bucket is idle capacity worth investigating.

Trends · Help

Unlike Overview, this tab reasons across every scan taken of the same save — the more scans you've run, the more history each chart has to show. With only one scan recorded, the trajectory chart shows a single centred dot instead of a line, and the Recent Changes feed tells you to scan again later to start building history.

Empire Trajectory

One line chart tracking a single metric across all your scans — switch what it plots with the toggle above it: Net Worth, Credits, Station Cash, Stations, Ships, or Reputation. The headline above the chart shows the latest value plus the change since the previous scan, coloured green for an increase and red for a decrease. The Ships metric has its own Total/Kills/Losses switch in the chart's left margin: Total is cumulative fleet size, while Kills and Losses are counts for that scan interval only — their headline reads as "this scan," not a running delta. Hover any point for the scan number, real date, value and delta; on Kills it also lists which factions credited the kills and any reputation change that came with them, and on Losses it lists the specific hulls lost that scan (capped at 8, with a "+N more" note beyond that).

Reputation

A separate chart, one coloured line per faction, plotted against in-game time rather than scan number (Xenon and Kha'ak are left out since standing with them never changes). Its own toggle switches between Change (default — every faction is rebased to zero at its first scan, so you're comparing relative movement) and Level (absolute standing). The vertical scale auto-fits whichever factions are currently visible, and the legend chips below the chart are clickable — toggle a faction's line on or off. Hovering a point shows absolute standing and tier, plus the change since the last scan and since the first.

Trade Totals · All-Time

Six cumulative cards: Trade Profit (net credits, green if non-negative else red), Sold, Bought, Volume Traded (units), Mining Value, and Internal Value (transfers between your own stations). These are running totals since your very first scan of this save, not per-scan figures.

Recent Changes

A newest-first feed grouped by scan, covering stations built or lost, ships acquired or lost, faction reputation crossing into a new named tier, milestones, and force build-ups — a hostile faction massing ships in a sector over several consecutive scans, shown with its growth multiplier and how many scans it's been climbing. This is the same signal the Military Advisor's Force Build-Up finding uses, surfaced here as history rather than a live warning.

Player Stations · Help

Every station you own gets one card. A 3-position slider on each card — Overview / Economy / More — swaps what the card shows; there's no separate full-page inspector, everything lives in the card itself.

Overview

A Sunlight % strip (solar stations only), Hull % and Shields % bars — coloured green/amber/red by how damaged they are, and Shields reads "No Shields" if the scan couldn't capture station shield data — then a four-cell stats row: Modules (hover for a breakdown by category), Crew (not yet tracked, shown as "—"), Ships (fleet whose home base is this station, hover splits Traders/Miners/Combat/Other — this counts ships assigned here, not ships physically docked right now), and Storage (% full, coloured by fill, hover breaks it down per cargo type). Below that, a Production tab lists every ware made here with a stockpile bar, units in stock, an "internal use" bar for how much other modules on the same station consume, hourly output, and a countdown to running dry on the limiting input (red under 1h, amber under 2h); a Docked tab lists ships currently docked, clickable through to Fleet.

Economy

A dropdown switches between a Breakdown view (a budget pie chart plus a cash-flow graph) and a Logs view (a running list of this station's trade and mining transactions).

More

Currently just Production: a ware-flow diagram showing how this station's modules feed each other, input to output.

NPC Stations · Help

This is a table, not a card grid — it lists every NPC station within a jump range you control with the slider above it (0–5 jumps), filterable by an All/Tradable toggle (Tradable drops any owner you're Hostile with) and a multi-select faction dropdown. Large empires can see well over a thousand reachable stations, so the table only builds rows near what's actually on screen as you scroll.

The table

Station, Owner (a coloured faction badge), Sector, and Type — sortable by clicking any header. Owner is the one thing the Player Stations tab doesn't have, since those are all yours by definition.

Station Inspector

Click a row to open its inspector in place of the table. It shows the owner faction, your reputation tier with them, station type, and jump distance, then a Wares list with an All/Buy/Sell filter: each ware shows a ▲ (buying) or ▼ (selling) arrow, an illegal/black-market tag where relevant, and the actual buy/sell price in credits — this is the price data the Player Stations tab can't show, since the scanner only reads NPC stations' posted offers. A Nearby Alternatives table lists, per ware, the next-closest station (NPC or your own) trading it the same direction, with jump distance — useful for checking whether you're getting the best deal reachable. A trade history table below that lists past transactions with this specific station.

Resource Library · Help

A read-only wiki of every hull and equipment macro the game ships, read from the same catalogue the Ship Builder draws on. It's only reachable via the sidebar's Resource Library flyout (Hulls, Weapons, Turrets, Shields, Engines, Thrusters, Software, Items) — nothing on another page links here directly except a fitted-hull jump from a Fleet ship.

Hulls

The only category with a 3-way List / Inspector / Comparison switch. List has a Faction/Size/Type filter row (options are built from whatever's actually in your catalogue) and shows Name, Faction badge, Class/Size, Hull HP, Price, and hardpoint counts (weapon/turret/shield icons), tagging anything you can't buy outright as "Capture Only." Clicking a row opens the Inspector: a stat card (type, size, hull HP, price, crew, cargo m³, missile/unit storage, cargo-type tags) followed by collapsible, size-grouped lists of every compatible item for each hardpoint slot with its own stats and price. If you jumped in from a specific ship's fitted loadout, a "Fitted" block sits above the catalogue showing what that ship actually has mounted, so you can compare it against everything else that fits.

Weapons, Turrets, Shields, Engines, Thrusters

Each gets its own List/Comparison toggle and a sortable table — Name, Faction, Size, then stat columns specific to that equipment class (Damage/Range/Rate for weapons, Capacity/Recharge/ Delay for shields, and so on), plus Price. Rows carry hover tooltips with the full stat breakdown.

Software & Items

Placeholders for now — the scan doesn't read these macros from the game files yet, so both categories show an empty-state message instead of a table.

Ships · Help

The Player sub-tab is always present; one extra sub-tab appears per NPC faction that actually has ships in the scan, largest fleet first. Clicking any header sorts the table by that column — click again to reverse.

Code / Name

The ship's name and code. If its fitted equipment can be resolved, the name is clickable straight into its Designs-tab card, and hovering it shows the full loadout tooltip.

Hull Type

A faction hull badge plus role icon and role text. Clickable into the Resource Library's Hull Inspector when the hull macro is in the catalogue.

Hull / Shields

Two independently sortable bars sharing one column. Hull colours smoothly from green through amber to red as it drops (the ramp is weighted so it already reads amber-ish around 80%, not just near empty), and turns blue above 100% (overheal). Shields uses fixed bands instead: blue at 80%+, amber at 50%–79%, red below that. A ship with no shield data shows a raw HP number or a dim "—" instead of a bar.

Order

Current behaviour order, coloured/iconed to match the Overview tab's Fleet by Order table. A courier actively in flight shows "Delivering" instead of its literal order, with a tooltip giving the cargo, ware, and destination.

Sector & Station

Sector links to that sector on the Sectors tab; Station links to the ship's homebase on the Stations tab, or shows a dim "—" if it isn't assigned one.

Pilot

A 0–5 star rating (half-star steps) of the pilot's piloting skill, not their name — hover for the name and full skill breakdown, click to jump to that pilot on the Crew tab.

NPC faction tabs

The same table with only five columns — Name/Code, Size, Hull Type, Order, Sector — since NPC ships don't carry pilot skill, homebase, or (usually) shield data. Sorting works the same way, tracked separately per faction so switching tabs doesn't reset another faction's sort.

Designs · Help

Every player ship with resolvable equipment is grouped by its exact design signature — hull macro plus every fitted item and count — so two ships only merge into one card when they're genuinely identical builds. When multiple distinct designs share the same hull type, each gets a "Config A/B/…" suffix, lettered by descending ship count.

Filters & Sort

Size and Faction narrow which cards show; Faction's options are built from whatever hull_origin_name values actually appear in your fleet. Sort: Used By ranks the most-built design first; Size ranks smallest hull first; Faction and Type sort alphabetically; Cost ranks the most expensive design (hull price plus every fitted item's price × count) first.

A design card

A per-category equipment breakdown (Weapons, Turrets, Shields, Engine, Thruster), sub-grouped by mount size with a "fitted / capacity" fraction header per size and each item's own stat columns and price; a hull preview panel with wireframe, size, hull HP and hull cost; and a footer with the ship count (click to expand into clickable ship-code chips jumping back to Fleet) and the design's total cost. Cards collapse individually or all at once via the "Collapse All" toggle, and stay collapsed across re-renders.

Ship Builder · Help

Reached from the sidebar's "Ship Builder" item. Opens on a hull you already own if you own any, otherwise the first cataloged hull, with an equipment slot pre-selected.

The Save button is a placeholder for now — it just shows a message that persistence is coming, so nothing you build here is saved, compared, or exported yet.

Choosing a hull

An editable blueprint name field plus a hull dropdown grouped by faction, showing size and a "Capture Only" tag where the hull can't be bought outright. Switching hulls resets whatever was fitted and re-selects the first slot the new hull has room for.

Fitting equipment

The left pane lists every equipment category as boxes grouped by mount size, each fitted item with +/- steppers, and a "Fit <Size> <Slot> · N free" button for any size that still has capacity. Clicking that button opens the right pane — every cataloged item for that slot and size, sorted by price, filterable by maker faction. Clicking an item there adds one (or increments the count), and is blocked once that size's capacity is full. Hovering a weapon, turret, shield, or engine row shows its full stats, including an In-Game/True Stats toggle for weapon and turret derived combat numbers.

Design total

A running cost at the bottom — hull price plus every fitted item's price times its count — updating live as you fit or remove equipment.

Crew · Help

Five colour-coded sub-tabs — All, Managers, Pilots, Service, Marines — each with a live count badge. The roster table sorts by clicking any header; Skill defaults to descending (best first), everything else ascending.

Primary Skill

Shown as a 0–5 star rating in half-star steps, three skill points per star. Which skill counts as "primary" depends on the role — management for Managers, piloting for Pilots, engineering for Service, boarding for Marines — so the column always reflects the stat that matters most for what that crew member actually does.

Assigned To & Sector

Assigned To links to the crew member's ship or station (Naval or Stations tab); Sector links to the Sectors tab, or does nothing if that sector hasn't been discovered yet.

Crew file

Clicking a row opens a side panel with the full skill breakdown (primary skill first, stars alongside the raw point value), the same Assignment/Sector links as the table, and — when the scan captured it — a Profile block (Faction, Gender, Ethnicity, Variant). This is also where a pilot lands when you click through from a ship's Pilot column on the Fleet tab.

Diplomacy · Help

A dropdown (not a tab strip) switches between Player and every NPC faction found in the save, ordered by standing — allies first, hostile factions last. A separate "Matrix" button next to it swaps in a full faction-by-faction grid instead. Everything here is read-only reporting; there's nothing to edit.

Tier, Score & Bar

Every panel shows a coloured Tier badge (Allied, Friendly, Neutral, Hostile, At War), a signed Score coloured to match, and a horizontal Bar whose width scales with how far the score is from neutral — green and filling right for positive, red and filling left for negative. The Player panel additionally splits out Base standing and any active Booster (a temporary modifier); NPC-faction panels don't, since that split isn't tracked for anyone but you. A locked faction (one the game itself won't let standing change for) shows a lock icon and note instead.

Matrix

A full faction × faction grid — every cell is one pair's standing, coloured by tier with a hover tooltip for the exact number. Clicking a faction's row label jumps straight to that faction's own panel.

Universe Map · Help

A flat-top hex grid, one large hex per cluster. Each cluster is coloured by whichever faction owns the most sectors inside it — unowned/unclaimed clusters stay grey. Jump-gate connections are drawn as glowing lines between sectors, coloured by each end's owner.

Zoom detail levels

The map starts at cluster resolution and reveals more the further you zoom in: cluster name labels appear first, then sector sub-hexes nested inside each cluster, then individual sector name labels. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom — the map keeps a fixed slight 3D tilt throughout.

Interactive Map vs. Save Map

Interactive Map is a static reference view with fog of war lifted — every cluster and sector in the galaxy is shown regardless of what your pilot has actually found. Save Map restricts the map to sectors and gates your current save has actually discovered; anything beyond that is hidden or marked "Undiscovered."

Sectors · Help

Only sectors your save has discovered appear here. The left list sorts alphabetically (grouped by cluster) by default, or by Player Stations count, Sunlight, or any individual resource yield (Ore, Silicon, Ice, Hydrogen, Helium, Methane, Nividium).

Summary tags

Territory: "Your Territory," "Unclaimed," or "Hostile," based on the owning faction's reputation tier. Proximity: Core (0–1 jumps from a player asset), Frontier (2–5), or Remote (6+). Feature tags call out anything notable — Mining-Rich (a high/very-high yield resource present), High Sunlight (130%+), or Hub (4 or more gate/highway connections).

Your Stations & Your Ships

Your Stations is a clickable list jumping to each station's card. Your Ships is collapsed by default (click to expand) and summarizes ship counts by category — mining, trade, combat — with the full per-ship list (name, role, code) available once opened.

Resources & Connections

Resources lists every ware this sector can produce, its yield level, and the current rechargeable amount. Connections lists neighbouring discovered sectors, whether the link is a gate or a highway, and the jump cost. A Station Presence list below that shows how many stations each NPC faction holds here.

Alerts · Help

Fully recomputed on every scan — there's no dismiss or acknowledge action, so an alert simply stops appearing once the underlying condition clears. Shows a single green "No alerts detected" message when none of the fourteen below are true.

Hostile Presence

Sourced from the Military advisor's own force comparison (theirs vs. your present defence, per sector) — only shows a sector when the hostiles there are at least a match for you. Undefended/Outmatched (you'd lose that fight) render red; Contested (could go either way) renders amber. A sector your defence would win ("Covered") never appears here.

Force Build-Up

Sectors where hostile combat strength has risen every tracked scan — staging, not a raid, which a single snapshot can't tell apart from a raid. Also sourced from the Military advisor.

Composition Gap

Sectors where the hostile force is mostly S/M strike craft and only a small share of your defenders' firepower can track targets that small — a loadout mismatch (add flak or fighter escorts), not a fight you're currently losing. Also sourced from the Military advisor.

Outranged

Sectors where hostile capital hulls (L/XL) hold a real weapon- range advantage over your whole defence there — a standoff- bombardment risk, taking fire the entire approach before you can reply. Also sourced from the Military advisor.

Damaged Fleet

Combat ships below 75% hull while undocked (docked ships are assumed to already be getting repaired), so every ship here has taken 25%+ damage. Severity is relative, not absolute — under 40% hull (more damage taken than hull remaining) renders red; 40-75% hull (25-60% damage) renders amber. Also sourced from the Military advisor.

Station Damaged / Under Attack

Read straight from each station's hull/shield health, not an advisor finding — no force comparison or trend behind it. Stations still Under Construction are excluded, since a new build naturally starts below full hull. Under 90% hull renders amber; under 60% hull renders red, as does a station whose shields have collapsed to near-zero while it still has a hull to protect — that's fire landing right now, ahead of the hull damage showing up on the next scan. One row per severity tier, listing the affected station codes.

Production Stalling

Read straight from each station's per-ware production runtime, not an advisor finding — no market-value filter behind it, so a ware with no NPC price still gets a warning. A line that's already out of its limiting input, or whose storage bay is already full, renders red; one due to hit either limit within a few hours renders amber. One row per station per severity, listing the affected wares.

Input Starvation

Read straight from each station's per-input consumption rate, not an advisor finding. This is the station's true combined draw on a raw input across every production line that shares it, rather than one line's own runway — Production Stalling can be optimistic when two lines draw on the same input. Under 2 hours remaining renders red; under 6 hours renders amber. One row per station per severity, listing the starving wares with their own remaining hours.

Station Underfunded

Read straight from each station's account balance against its own estimated supply budget (roughly 2 hours of its input costs), not an advisor finding. Stations with nothing budgeted (pure-output builds with no inputs to restock) are excluded. Balance below about 10% of that budget means the station can't afford to restock itself. One row listing the affected station codes with their current balances.

Idling Ships

Any ship currently on the "Waiting" order — idle capacity doing nothing. Lists up to 6 ship codes plus a "+N more" count beyond that.

Idle Miners

The subset of waiting ships whose role is a mining role, broken out as its own alert since an idle miner is usually a more direct loss than an idle trader.

Storage Overflow

Stations about to hit cargo capacity on a surplus ware within the next hour, so the production backing up behind that cap starts going to waste. Under 30 minutes renders red. Sourced from the Economic advisor's overflow-risk findings (which itself flags anything within 5h) — the single source of truth for this, rather than a separate raw cargo-percentage check.

Stranded Deliveries

A ship has been holding pickup cargo with no delivery destination assigned for longer than the stranded-delivery threshold — orders that need re-checking, not lost cargo. Sourced from the Trader advisor's stranded-delivery findings, the single source of truth for this rather than a separate raw-order check.

Under-Equipped Ships

A combat-role ship (fighter, corvette, frigate, destroyer or gunboat) with hardpoints for weapons or turrets but nothing fitted in them, or shield hardpoints with no shields fitted — read straight from each ship's loadout against its hull's slot layout, not an advisor finding. Ships still under construction are excluded, since they haven't been fitted out yet by design. One row listing the affected ship codes, each tagged with its specific gap ("no weapons" and/or "no shields").

The topbar's Alerts badge is a coarser count than this tab's list — it adds at most one point each for hostile presence, force build-up, composition gap, outranged, idling ships, damaged fleet, station damage, production stalling, input starvation, station underfunded, storage overflow, stranded deliveries and under-equipped ships (max 13 total), regardless of how many sectors, ships or stations are actually involved, so a badge of "2" just means two of those thirteen conditions are currently true.

Events · Help

A flat, newest-first feed across seven categories — Alerts, Upkeep, Missions, Diplomacy, News, Tips, and Uncategorised — each with its own icon and colour. A filter chip strip lets you narrow to one category or view them all together.

A row

Icon and colour by category, a title (or the first line of the event text if there's no separate title), a faction badge where one applies, and how long ago it happened.

Hover for detail

Hovering a row opens the full picture: both relative and absolute in-game time, the complete event text, the faction's current reputation tier/score/bar if one applies, a sector section (ship/station counts there) when the event names a resolvable location, and — for events tied to a specific ship or station — that entity's hull/shield bars. Rows are hover-only; there's no click-through to another tab from here.

Missions · Help

Every station-board mission the scan found, grouped into a card per offering station (name and sector shown on the card itself) and sorted alphabetically by station name.

A mission row

A type icon (Find, Repair, Transport, Deliver, Trade, Escort, Rescue, Destroy, Fight, Protect, Intelligence, Drop, Build, Board, or Scan), the mission name and a one-line description, a colour-coded difficulty badge (very easy through very hard), and the reward — credits, a text reward, or both.

Type filter

Chips for every mission type actually present in the data, each with its own count, plus an "All" chip. Hovering a mission shows its full objectives list and distance from the player.

Economic Advisor · Help

The coloured bar on the left of each card is the priority rail. Its fill height is that finding's priority score relative to the single highest-scoring finding in the current scan — so a full rail is the most urgent thing found, and a short rail is comparatively minor. The rail stays fixed to the full findings list even when you filter by type, so a card's height never shifts just because you switched tabs. Sort order and rail height always agree: read top-to-bottom for rank, or glance at the rails for how big the gap is between one finding and the next.

Overflow Risk

Flags a station whose production surplus will fill its cargo bay within 24 in-game hours. If a station makes more of something than it consumes or ships out, that surplus piles up in storage — once it caps, the excess production is simply wasted (nothing more can be stored). Priority is weighted by both the value being produced and how soon it caps, so an urgent, high-value overflow ranks above a slow, low-value one.

Market Opportunity

Matches a surplus ware at one of your stations against a nearby NPC station that genuinely wants more of it (its buy offer's desired stock target exceeds what it currently has — not just "willing to buy," but "actively short"). Ranked by estimated credits/hour, discounted by how many jumps away the buyer is — a big opportunity three jumps out scores lower than a smaller one next door.

Pricing Gap

Compares what you're currently charging on your own posted sell offers against what a reachable NPC station is paying for the same ware. If someone nearby pays more than your listed price, this flags the gap and estimates the one-time gain if you rerouted your current stock there instead.

Idle Hauler

Catches a Freighter/Transport sitting nearly empty (under 15% cargo fill) while its home station is actively overproducing something. The ship has capacity, the station has product — the mismatch is the finding. Priority scales with how much capacity is sitting unused.

Military Advisor · Help

The coloured bar on the left of each card is the priority rail, and it works exactly as on the Economic tab — fill height is the finding's score relative to the single highest-scoring military finding this scan. Military scores are threat points (ship counts weighted by combat role, verdict and distance), a different unit from the economy's credits/hour — which is why each tab is ranked separately and rail heights are never comparable across the two.

A card showing a swords icon carries counter advice: hover it for weapon and refit suggestions matched to that particular force's make-up — strike-craft swarms, capital hulls, and shield-heavy versus hull-heavy fleets each get their own counsel.

Hostile Presence verdicts
Undefended

You have nothing armed in the sector — anything you own there is exposed.

Outmatched

Simulating the fight both ways, they break your defence at least twice as fast as you could break theirs (or your ships can't hurt them at all).

Contested

The engagement could go either way. Fleets the scan couldn't assess also land here — missing data is never scored as harmless.

Covered

Your present force should hold without reinforcement.

Hostile Presence

Flags hostile-faction ships operating in a sector where you own a station, within reach of your position. Hostility is decided by reputation (−25 or below), so a faction you've personally angered counts just as much as the Xenon. Both sides' fitted loadouts are compared — sustained damage output against hull and shield pools — to produce the verdict above. Priority scales with ship count (a combat hull counts four times a freighter), worsens with the verdict, and falls off with distance.

Tracking Mismatch

Their force is mostly strike craft (S/M hulls), while most of your defenders' firepower physically can't track targets that small — plasma turrets never connect with fighters; flak, pulse and guided missiles do. Fires only when the mismatch is one-sided: at least 60% of their ships are small against less than 25% of your damage able to hit them, and fewer than 4 craft never counts as a swarm.

Outranged

Hostile capital ships (L/XL) out-reach every defender you have in the sector by 25% or more — the standoff-bombardment setup, where your ships take fire for the whole approach before they can reply. Only capital hulls count on their side: a fighter with a long-range gun still has to close, but a destroyer parks at maximum range and pounds. Priority is the kilometres of standoff times the platforms that can exploit it.

Force Build-Up

Hostile hardware value in a sector has risen every scan for at least three scans and at least doubled overall. A raid arrives, fights and leaves — only staging climbs steadily, so this is the early warning for an invasion. It's also the one military finding not gated by distance: the threat is to the station in that sector, which doesn't get less threatened because you flew away. Priority scales with fleet size and how fast it's growing.

Damaged Ship

A combat ship flying below 75% hull without being docked anywhere. Shields regenerate on their own; hull damage persists between fights, so it's the signal that a repair stop is overdue. Priority is the missing hull points in absolute terms — a battered destroyer outranks a battered fighter because it costs more to lose and takes longer to replace. Docked damaged ships are assumed to be already handled.

Trader Advisor · Help

The priority rail works the same as the Economic tab — fill height is a finding's score relative to the single highest-scoring trader finding this scan. Unlike Economic (which reacts to one station's current surplus or cargo state), these five rules reason at the galaxy or fleet level: where to expand, where the biggest cross-sector spread is, and whether the fleet and bank balance are actually being put to work.

Station Siting

Flags a discovered sector with a mineable resource deposit and no player station anywhere in it yet. Fires regardless of demand — low demand for the resource is not a reason to skip the suggestion — but calls out reachable unmet NPC demand explicitly when it's strong, so you know when a build is backed by a ready market versus a bet on future use.

Galaxy Arbitrage

Finds the largest reachable buy/sell spread for a ware across NPC stations galaxy-wide — independent of anything you currently own or stock. Both the cheap seller and the paying buyer must be within reach and on speaking terms with you; priority discounts for the total round-trip distance.

Stranded Delivery

A courier has been holding cargo it picked up with no resolved delivery destination for an unusually long time. Priority weighs both how long it's been stranded and the value of the cargo it's sitting on.

Idle Trade Capital
